Research

I work on the syntax of case, agreement and alignment in Iranian languages, and on how language models learn grammatical structure.

Dissertation

Alignment in Human Language and Large Language Models: Evidence from Iranian Languages

My dissertation brings my theoretical and computational work together. It examines split alignment in Iranian languages, where case and agreement patterns change with tense and aspect, and asks whether large language models learn these patterns in the way human grammars organize them. By comparing the generalizations found in Kurdish varieties and related languages with what LLMs represent and produce, it uses alignment as a test case for how much grammatical structure these models acquire.

Committee: Andrew Carnie and Simin Karimi (co-chairs), Heidi Harley, Mihai Surdeanu, Faruk Akkuş and Mohsen Mahdavi Mazdeh

Research interests

Theoretical linguistics

Alignment and argument structure

My primary research investigates split alignment in Sorani Kurdish varieties such as Ardalani. I explore how case and agreement patterns are conditioned by tense and aspect, arguing for a model where these features are dissociated in the syntactic structure. This includes work on stative passives and what they reveal about the argument structure of the verb.

The syntax–morphology interface

I study how syntactic structures are realized morphologically. Working in Distributed Morphology, I analyze clitic placement, head movement and morpheme ordering as post-syntactic operations, including the timing of Vocabulary Insertion, the factors behind allomorphy, and the internal structure of derived nouns.

Human language technology

Tokenization for LLMs Current

Tokenization decides the units a model learns from, and so shapes everything it represents afterward. I ask how linguistically informed units, such as morphemes, affect what models learn, especially for morphologically complex languages.

Morpheme-based embeddings

In my M.S. work, I designed and implemented a morpheme-based embedding model in PyTorch for low-resource, morphologically complex languages. Building word representations from morphemes improved performance on downstream NLP tasks, with Sorani Kurdish as a case study.

LLMs and language acquisition

I use large language models as cognitive models, asking how their learning trajectories and error patterns can shed light on the stages of first language acquisition in children.

NLP tools for Sorani Kurdish Current

I am building a rule-based morphological segmenter for Sorani Kurdish that checks each proposed split against the corpus, along with a cleaned corpus of Sorani Kurdish collected from Wikipedia.
